# Harbor Studio business plan

## Executive synthesis

Harbor Studio is a fictional business used to test long-form document design. Every customer count, price, date and financial figure in this plan is an illustrative assumption. None describes Rōvn or a real operating company.

The proposed service helps small training organisations maintain a consistent set of learner guides, facilitator notes, checklists and evaluation forms. The customer currently updates these files independently. A course change may reach the slide deck while an old version remains in the participant workbook. The service would maintain one approved content model and generate the appropriate files for each reader.

The initial plan targets 12 active customer sites at the end of year one. The illustrative recurring price is $500 per site per month. At that year-end run rate, annualised subscription revenue would be $72,000. This is a run-rate calculation, not a forecast of revenue earned during the first year. Customer activation timing determines recognised revenue.

The immediate decision is whether the service can reduce correction effort without creating a new approval bottleneck. A bounded pilot should test that question before the team adds more document families or customer segments.

## Customer need and current alternatives

The intended buyer is an operations manager who owns a recurring training programme and coordinates changes across several instructors. The reader of a learner workbook has different needs from the person maintaining an instructor guide. One needs clear explanations and practice space; the other needs timing, facilitation notes and exception handling.

The working hypothesis is that repeated copying between files creates avoidable mistakes. The plan does not assume the buyer wants a new authoring application. The first implementation accepts the customer's existing approved material and returns files that can be reviewed in familiar tools.

The main alternatives are a shared folder with manual editing, a learning-management system's built-in editor, and a specialist design agency. The shared folder is easy to adopt but relies on careful coordination. A learning platform can manage publication but may not produce the required offline documents. An agency can provide strong design but may be expensive for frequent small revisions. These are hypotheses to test with customer evidence.

| Alternative | Useful strength | Question to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared folder | Familiar editing and ownership | How are outdated copies found and retired? |
| Learning platform | Central online access | Which offline and editable exports are required? |
| Design agency | Specialist visual execution | What is the cost and delay for a small approved change? |

## Product and delivery model

The pilot delivers three coordinated artifacts: a participant guide, an instructor guide and an operational checklist. The underlying content has stable identifiers. A change to a shared fact must be visible in every affected artifact before release. The system never adds an example, claim or policy simply to fill an empty design component.

Each engagement begins with an inventory of approved sources and a named customer reviewer. The team records which files are current, which contain unresolved conflicts and which must remain in their original format. An unresolved policy conflict stays visible. It cannot be resolved by choosing whichever wording fits a page more neatly.

The workflow includes source intake, structural preparation, customer review, generation, quality checks and delivery. Human review focuses on meaning and ownership. Automated checks focus on missing content, invalid links, numerical consistency and layout failures. The plan assumes that the customer retains final approval of its training content.

The service excludes learning accreditation, legal advice, medical guidance and changes to the customer's assessment criteria. Those exclusions are illustrative scope boundaries for this fictional plan. They do not establish any real commercial offer.

## Market-entry experiment

The first experiment uses three synthetic course packs to establish a reproducible baseline. Each pack includes a short guide, a longer guide and a checklist with dense rows. The test records preparation time, review corrections, export failures and the number of source changes that must be applied manually in more than one place.

If the internal workflow is reliable, a separately authorised customer trial could test whether the output fits an actual organisation's process. A successful trial would require the customer's acceptance of the files and evidence of reduced correction effort. A visually attractive sample alone would not establish demand or time savings.

The plan deliberately distinguishes acquisition hypotheses from delivery capacity. Increasing the number of interested organisations does not mean that the service can support them. The team must know how much reviewer and operator time each active site consumes before setting a launch volume.

## Operations, ownership and milestones

The example programme owner maintains the source inventory and resolves scope questions with the customer. An editor prepares the content model. A designer maintains the shared layouts. A reviewer checks source preservation and usability. One person may hold several roles, but each review action still needs an accountable owner.

| Milestone | Acceptance evidence | Dependency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source inventory | Every file has an owner, status and intended output | Complete input pack |
| First coordinated pack | All three artifacts preserve approved content | Reviewed content model |
| Quality review | No missing content, broken navigation or visible overflow | Generated files |
| Revision exercise | A changed source fact updates each affected artifact | Stable identifiers |
| Pilot decision | Recorded benefits, costs and unresolved limitations | Complete test record |

The service must support a recoverable release. Approved source versions and generated artifacts remain associated. If a new layout introduces an error, the team can reproduce the previous approved output without reconstructing it from screenshots. A release receipt records source version, renderer version, output hashes and review status.

## Economics and scenario assumptions

The following schedule uses illustrative year-end active-site counts. It compares annualised subscription run rates, not earned first-year revenue. Every scenario uses the same $500 monthly price solely to make the arithmetic inspectable.

| Scenario | Active sites | Monthly price per site | Monthly run rate | Annualised run rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lower volume | 6 | $500 | $3,000 | $36,000 |
| Base planning case | 12 | $500 | $6,000 | $72,000 |
| Higher volume | 18 | $500 | $9,000 | $108,000 |

The illustrative annual direct-delivery budget is $30,000. Shared operating expense is assumed to be $24,000. Against the base annualised run rate of $72,000, these assumptions leave $18,000 before taxes, financing, capital spending and other unmodelled costs. This arithmetic is not a profitability forecast: timing, churn, discounts and actual labour can change the result materially.

The working model should store price, volume and cost assumptions as inputs. Summary tables in this document should be checked against the workbook. A reader must be able to change an input and inspect the resulting calculations. A spreadsheet containing only pictures of financial tables would not meet that requirement.

## Risks, limits and next decision

The first risk is that the service adds process without reducing work. The pilot therefore measures total human effort, including review and corrections. The second risk is that source material is less structured than the samples. The test set must include long names, uneven headings, wide tables, missing optional fields and conflicting version labels.

The third risk is export divergence. A DOCX can reflow in a recipient's editor; a slide can substitute a missing font; a workbook can display a stale formula result. The output record must distinguish content checks from rendering checks and identify which application was used for verification.

The next decision follows the evidence: extend the trial, narrow its scope or stop. The example steering group should receive a short decision memo supported by the complete test record. It should not need to read this full plan again to identify the requested action.
